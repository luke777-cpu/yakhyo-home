Recording medication response by splitting it into just ON and OFF has been used for a long time. It's simple, and easy to put into words. But **there is a stretch this approach doesn't capture well**.

The graph above is an example. The solid line does rise, but it stops at about half height and comes back down from there. If you had to pick one of two boxes for this day, neither one fits well. **It's neither no effect at all nor the usual full effect**. So a day like this either gets left out of the record entirely, or gets marked toward one side on a whim, and later becomes unreadable.

Recording by degree keeps this day intact, as it actually was. Once a single peak number accumulates over several days, you can see how many times it was low, and at which dose. **Measuring, not choosing** — that's why we use the word "output."

It also matters that you don't have to judge it. You don't have to decide for yourself whether right now is a good state or a bad one — **just record how much**, and the curve will show you, later, where that moment stood.
