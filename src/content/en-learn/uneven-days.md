faq:
  - q: Is being uneven itself a problem?
    a: Day-to-day differences happen to everyone. What matters at a clinic visit is how large that difference is, and whether it repeats at a particular time of day or under particular conditions. That's why several days of records matter more than a single day's.
  - q: Is it okay to record only the bad days?
    a: If only bad days are left, there's nothing to compare them against. You need the good days alongside them to see what was different. The record of the good days is, in fact, the more important half.
  - q: How many days of records does it take before a pattern shows up?
    a: Three days is enough to start seeing something; a week is usually enough to tell whether it's a repeating pattern or not. It's better to keep a few days going without a gap than to try to record every detail perfectly.

The two curves above are **two different days on which the same medication was taken at the same time**. The dose markers sit in exactly the same spot. But the peaks around midday and evening are noticeably different.

After a day like this, you remember it as "the medication didn't work today." Then, at the next visit, you say, "it's been up and down lately." That's true, but it leaves the clinician with little to go on in deciding what to look at.

This is where keeping records changes things. Once several days are placed side by side, the questions change.

- Do the bad days cluster around **the same time of day**?
- Do the bad days share a **common condition**?
- How much did the actual dose time shift?
- How many of the days were good ones?

**A single day's curve reflects that day's circumstances; several days' curves reflect a pattern.** One recording can't tell coincidence from repetition. If the same thing shifts in the same place three times, it stops being coincidence — and that's the point where the conversation at a clinic visit can begin.

So this article recommends just one thing. Rather than recording today in great detail, try **keeping a record for several days without a gap**. It's fine if you note only a few items.
