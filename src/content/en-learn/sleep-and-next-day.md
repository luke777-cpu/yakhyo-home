This is the same person's same morning dose. The only difference is the night before. The solid line comes up a little later, and even after it rises, it does not go as high as the dotted line.

There is a part of this picture that is easy to misread. **A lower curve does not mean "the medication got weaker."** On a day with too little sleep, the same amount of movement can feel heavier, so the output that ends up in the record tends to come out lower. And that, too, is information about the body on that day.

Sleep is one of the easiest conditions to record. **The time you fell asleep, how many times you woke up, and the time you woke up.** Three lines are enough. If you add the time of the first morning dose on top of that, a few days later you can line up a morning after good sleep next to a morning after poor sleep.

Once you gather a few days like that, "the medication doesn't seem to be working well lately, maybe because I'm not sleeping" turns into "four nights out of the week I woke up three or more times, and the following mornings the first dose took longer to come up." In the clinic, these two statements are very different.
