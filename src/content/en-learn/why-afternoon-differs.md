In the curve above, all four doses are spaced at the same interval. Yet the peaks get lower toward the afternoon. On a day like this, it's easy to feel that "the afternoon dose is weak."

But the medication is the same. What changes is **the state of the body the medication enters**. In the morning, the stomach is usually empty and the body is less tired. In the afternoon, there's food from lunch, activity has built up, and the interval to the next dose may have been pushed back.

There's one distinction worth making here.

- **A lower peak** — the afternoon peak itself is lower
- **A shorter duration** — the height is similar, but it drops before the next dose

The two look different on the curve, and they call for different conversations at a clinic visit. By feel, both get lumped together as "sagging in the afternoon," so without a record they cannot be told apart.

So for a few days, try recording the time and the output **for every dose**. Line the days up, and where in the afternoon the curve drops becomes visible. If that point is similar every day, that is the first thing to show at your clinic visit.
