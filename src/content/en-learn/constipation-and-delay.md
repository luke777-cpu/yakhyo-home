Two things stand out in this curve. **The point where it starts to rise is pushed back significantly, and even after the late rise, it doesn't reach as high as the dotted line.** This is a shape that often appears together on days the stomach and intestines are moving slowly.

Constipation is very common in Parkinson's disease, and in some cases it starts even before the motor symptoms do. Because of that, it's easy to set it aside as "a separate discomfort unrelated to the medication" — but when you look at the path the medication has to travel to be absorbed, it's on the same road.

What to record is simple. **One yes or no mark per day.** Add a single number for "how many days since" and that's enough. Then place next to it the time it took the medication to come up that day.

After a few days, laying the two lines side by side shows whether the curve was pushed back on every day without a bowel movement, or whether it fluctuated regardless. Either way, that gives you one more thing to talk about at your clinic visit.
