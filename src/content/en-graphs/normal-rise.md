A single curve holds four pieces of information: **when it started to rise, how high it climbed, how long it held, and how it came down.**

Being able to look at these four things separately is what makes the graph useful. A statement like "the medication worked well today" bundles all four together, making it hard to tell what was good and what fell short.

Every other shape that comes later is this same shape with **just one of these four changed**. That's why getting familiar with this shape first makes all the others much easier to read.
