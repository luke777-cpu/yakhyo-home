Looking at one peak as a whole, it just registers as "a stretch when you moved well." But different things actually happen in **the rising stretch, near the top, and the descending stretch.**

Where dyskinesia falls among these three differs from person to person. Some people describe it as clustering near the top, and others describe it as appearing on the way down. **You can't tell from the curve alone — the position only shows up when you record event times alongside it.**

So when reading this shape, look at the curve and the event marks together. Once several days build up, you can see whether the marks cluster at the same spot on the peak or scatter around. If that spot stays consistent, what you can tell your doctor becomes much more specific — not Saying, "I have dyskinesia," but Saying, "It shows up every time, about 30 minutes after my best time."
