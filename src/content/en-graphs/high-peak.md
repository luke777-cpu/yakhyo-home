A high peak by itself is **neither good nor bad.** It might mean a stretch when you moved well, or it might mean other discomfort was present around that same stretch. The curve alone doesn't tell you which one it is.

That's why, for this shape, **what's recorded near the peak** matters. A peak of the same height means something completely different depending on whether it comes with "Saying I walked best at this time" or with "Saying my body was shaking at this time."

The other thing to look at in the shape is the **width**. As in the illustration above, a peak that climbs steeply is usually narrow. It often doesn't stay up for long in proportion to how high it climbed, and comes back down quickly, so looking only at the peak height can make it look better than it really was. You need to also record **the time you started feeling heavy again**, along with the good time, for the width to show up.
