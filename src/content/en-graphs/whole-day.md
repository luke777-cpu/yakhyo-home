Looking at a whole day as one line shows **things you can't see peak by peak** — the difference between morning and afternoon, where the low stretches fall, and the rhythm of the day as a whole.

The first thing to look at is **where the low stretches fall**. What you take away is completely different depending on whether the low stretches always come right before the next dose, or are scattered without regard to time of day. In the first case, you're looking at the relationship between hold time and dosing interval. In the second, you're also looking at what was different about that particular day.

A day's curve tells you more when you line up several days side by side. What looks like just that day's circumstances when you look at only one day reveals, once three or four days build up, **whether it's a repeating pattern or not**. When you bring records to a visit, several days' worth is far more useful than just one day's.
