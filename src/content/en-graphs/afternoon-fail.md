This shape is read as **a row of peaks, not a single peak**. You scan from left to right and find where the height turns.

In the illustration above, the first two peaks rise high, but the last two are noticeably lower. The spacing between dose times stays the same all day. It's the same medication taken at the same intervals, but **only the afternoon peaks stay low**.

There's an order to reading it. First, check whether **the spacing between dose marks is even**. If the afternoon dose was taken late, what looks low might just not have come up yet. Once you've checked the spacing, find **from which dose onward** the peak height turns.

"The medication doesn't work as well in the afternoon" is a common way to put it. Translated into a curve, that becomes a specific sentence like this: starting from the third dose, the peak settles at about half height. The amount of information carried into a clinic visit is completely different.
