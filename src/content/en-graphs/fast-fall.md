A peak tells **two different stories on its left and right sides.** The left side is about absorption and the rise; the right side is about how long it holds and how it comes down. This shape is a case where **the left side looks normal, but only the right side has changed.**

So if you only record the height of the peak, this shape stays invisible. In the graph above, the solid line and the dotted line climb to almost the same height. As far as "today's dose worked well" goes, the two days look identical. The difference is what comes after.

There's one thing the record needs — **the time it started to feel heavy again.** Without this value, the whole right side of the peak is left blank, and a day that worked well stays indistinguishable from a day that worked well but dropped off quickly.

A steep fall once is different from a steep fall for several days in a row. If this shape repeats over several days, then it belongs together with the shape where the held stretch keeps getting shorter. The related article below picks up from here.
